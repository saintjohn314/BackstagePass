SETTINGS & RULEBOOK

HOW TO RUN A VENUE

  1. Lease a space. Pay the signing deposit; rent is due every Monday. Bigger rooms unlock as your reputation grows.
  2. Design it in 3D. Fire code sets your legal capacity: every guest needs floor space (5 sq ft standing, 7 seated), restrooms cap occupancy, and fire exits must stay connected. Bars must start on a wall. Speakers flank the stage, lights go on it.
  3. Book a three-band bill. The biggest draw headlines. Genres that mix well (punk + metal, folk + indie) boost the crowd; clashing bills kill the vibe. Steady bands are reliable — erratic ones can steal the show or bomb.
  4. Set up the night. Pick the night (weekends draw more), price tickets near "fair" (gouging empties rooms), choose promo and security, and pay the headliner a guarantee or a share of the door.
  5. Run the show. Make calls when things go wrong. Bar sales are where venues really make money — if your bar is too small, lines form and the mood sours.
  6. Read the settlement. Great, packed nights grow your reputation; it unlocks bigger acts and bigger rooms. Run out of money on rent day and the landlord changes the locks.
  7. Chase awards. Thirty to win, from your first sold-out night to selling out the stadium. They fill the gold-record shelf in your office, and the awards card always shows your next goal.

Goal: sell out the Metro Mega Stadium and become a Living Legend.
